The DAR villa film: full plan

A scroll-driven website where one drone flight follows DAR building a modern villa in Lebanon, from the first drawing to the night the owner gets the keys. This page is the production plan. The 3D preview shows exactly the order, camera moves and text; the final site swaps the 3D for photoreal Higgsfield footage.

01The idea

DAR's promise is “Results First. Payment Follows.” The film proves it visually: you watch the result being built, stage by stage, and the website only asks for contact at the end, after the keys are handed over.

The logo is the story

The DAR circle is the pivot and the line turns around it from 0° to 180°: empty land at 0°, the villa delivered at 180°, the full turn from promise to result. The site uses this three times: the loading screen, the gauge that turns as you scroll (0° → 180°), and the final frame next to the finished villa.

04How the website plays it

The film section

  • Each clip is turned into a frame sequence (WebP/AVIF) so scrolling down plays it forward and scrolling up plays it backward, frame-exact, like a drone you steer.
  • Three sizes: 1920 px desktop, 1280 px laptop, 9:16 crop for phones. Frames load one scene ahead so the first screen is under 2 MB.
  • Text panels, the stage rail and the rising logo gauge stay on top, exactly as in the 3D preview.
  • At the end, the villa is explorable: point at the pool, parking, tennis court, basketball court or house for details.

After the film

  • Promised / Delivered: drag a divider across the screen, the plan on one side and the finished villa on the other. This carries the payment message.
  • Services: finishing, plumbing, electrical, design & project management.
  • Principles: what DAR believes in and refuses to do.
  • Partners: DCP and HEK.HOUSE.
  • Contact: WhatsApp-first form, phone, email. English, French and Arabic throughout.

05Keeping the villa identical in every shot

The biggest risk with AI video is the building changing shape between shots. The method:

  1. Master design sheet first. One approved set of the finished villa (front, side, top, dusk). Every keyframe prompt uses it as a reference image.
  2. Build backwards. Generate the finished villa first, then “remove” stages from that same image (finishing → walls → frame → slab → pit → land), so the plot, the angle and the neighbours never move.
  3. Chain the clips. The last frame of clip N is the first frame of clip N+1.
  4. Same lens, same light. One drone lens and a fixed sun direction for all daytime shots; only scene 9 changes to dusk.
  5. Lebanese checklist on every prompt. Concrete frame + hollow block, props and plywood, services in the floor, stone cladding, tanks and solar on roofs, no timber framing.

06Production steps & timeline

  1. You approve this plan and the moodboard. Any change to the villa or the shot list happens here, not after generation.
  2. Test (½ day). One keyframe (scene 6, pipes in the floor) and one clip. I measure the real credit cost and send you both for a realism check.
  3. Master villa sheet (½ day). The finished villa in 4 views. You approve the design.
  4. Keyframes (1–2 days). All 17, built backwards from the master. You approve them as a contact sheet.
  5. Clips (1–2 days). 17 drone moves, with retakes where the building drifts.
  6. Site build (1–2 days). Frames extracted and wired into the scroll, the Promised/Delivered section, final copy in EN/FR/AR.
  7. QA (½ day). Your laptop (4K), phones, slow connection, all three languages.
  8. Your review. You decide what goes to DAR. Nothing is published by me.

About 6–8 working days from approval, depending on how many retakes the AI needs.
